I couldn't find much genuine user feedback. Most of what I found is agency-written "best of" roundups, and their ratings come from small review samples. My second search failed, so I have no forum or Reddit discussions to draw on.

What the ratings show

Hook Agency has a 4.9 rating from 179 Google reviews, though that figure appears in a roundup that compares agencies, so treat it as a vendor-friendly summary.
HigherVisibility has a 4.6/5.0 from 16 Clutch reviews as of 09/2026. That sample is small.
Some agencies have almost no public feedback. One roundup notes that No public Clutch rating; profile currently has no reviews for one firm, and another says a provider has a Limited public aggregate rating.
Recurring themes in the reviews and comparisons

Trade specialization matters to contractors. Writers consistently favor agencies with construction or home-services track records over generalists. One guide advises prioritizing agencies with documented construction trade client work over generalists with no public trade experience.
Contractors want more than rankings. The features cited as most valuable are Google Business Profile management, service-area pages, citation consistency, and review collection. One roundup describes a firm that focuses on citation consistency, proximity-based ranking factors, and local review collection.
Bundled platforms appeal to some. Scorpion is recommended for contractors who want local SEO bundled with review management and call tracking under one login.
Cost and commitment are common drawbacks. One roundup notes that Hook's performance-driven model requires steady, ongoing ad and SEO budget commitments, and lists iLocal at $1,000+/month. Another says traditional agency models come with longer-term contracts.
DIY tools have limits. BrightLocal is described as a workhorse local SEO toolkit many agencies use for audits, rank tracking, and citations. Moz Local is described as not the tool for a sprawling franchise operation, which implies it suits smaller businesses.
Caveats

Many of these rankings are written by the agencies themselves or by competitors, so they may favor the author's clients.
Reviews are sparse for many firms, so a single high rating doesn't say much.
Pricing is often unpublished, so you'll need to request quotes.
If you want real customer experiences, check Google and Clutch reviews directly, and search Reddit communities for contractors or home-service marketing, which I couldn't access in this search. Asking a few local contractors who they use can also be useful.
